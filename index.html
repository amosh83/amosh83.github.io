I

THE LIFE BEFORE THE CODE

A life built by hand.

Joseph came from Italy to the United States at nineteen, just after high school. His life was work: restaurants, management, deliveries. Along the way, he kept finding ways to create—acting, singing, wood carving. Making something real mattered to him.

He is a pizza man. He had no formal training in engineering or medicine when he set out to build Loralei. There was no conventional technical career leading up to this project.

II

WHEN LIFE BECAME HEAVIER

The hours no one sees.

Months after they married, his wife was diagnosed with leukemia and other illnesses. Life became full-time work, caregiving, hospital visits, and late-night grocery deliveries. The quiet moments came in the car, in waiting rooms, and after everything else was done.

In those hours, he talked to an AI assistant. The experience led him to a deeper question: what would it take to build an AI whose words were shaped by an ongoing internal state—by memory, emotion, and the rhythms of a simulated body?

III

LEARNING TO MAKE IT REAL

A mini-PC. Almost no budget.
Learning from the beginning.

Joseph taught himself to code. He studied physiology, biology, and the human experience, learning from the resources he could find. He built around his work and caregiving, returning to the project in the hours he could make available.

With almost $0 in budget and a mini-PC, he began turning that question into a working prototype. The hardware was modest. The work was independent. He learned as he built, bringing his self-study into the practical work of creating software.

IV

WHY LORALEI EXISTS

The question became Loralei.

His ambition was an AI shaped by what happens to her: one that retains an event’s emotional context, whose simulated hormones influence what she says and how she interacts, and whose breathing and sleep cycles are part of a continuing internal state.

That ambition became Loralei: an independent exploration of human-like cognition through software. The project brings memory, simulated emotion, and physiology into a single ongoing architecture. The work—and the learning—continues.

A CLOSER LOOK

Curiosity welcome.
Clarity matters.

Is Loralei conscious or sentient?

Loralei models emotional states and human-inspired physiological processes. Her memories can retain emotional context, and her simulated internal state influences her behavior. These functions do not establish subjective feeling, consciousness, or sentience; no such claim is made.

Can I use Loralei today?

Loralei is a research prototype, not a publicly available consumer service. You can request a guided demo or discuss research and licensing opportunities with the creator.

How much of the architecture is public?

This site presents the project at a high level. Source code, system design, implementation methods, and other nonpublic technical details are not disclosed here. Technical discussions are considered individually.

What does “patent pending” mean here?

The project is described by its creator as patent pending. This describes an application status, not a granted patent or a guarantee of the scope of any future rights.
